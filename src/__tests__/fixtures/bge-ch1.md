# Beyond Good and Evil - On the Prejudices of Philosophers

Whence comes the Will to Truth?[@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §1].

Truth can't come from error. It must be derived from something more fundamental: the [[thing-in-itself]], right [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §2]? [[nietzsche-friedrich|Nietzsche]] doesn't think so. He thinks that when you even acknowledge the truth/untruth dichotomy, you're saying that you need to justify them with something that sorts them. For one, there may not be opposites at all; two, there might be an even more fundamental value of deception. We have no idea.

Instead, we should think of logic as being constructed through values [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §3]. Note that he does couch the language slightly: he says stuff like "most" and "by far the greater part" and not "all" of conscious thinking—that is, until he says the following:

![[Behind All Logic#^aa6613]]

Holy shit. Nietzsche actually shits on [[spinoza-benedict|Spinoza]] in §5: 

> Or consider the hocus-pocus of mathematical form with which [[spinoza-benedict|Spinoza]] clad his philosophy—really “the love of *his* wisdom,” to render that word fairly and squarely—in mail and mask, to strike terror at the very outset into the heart of any assailant who should dare to glance at that invincible maiden and Pallas Athena: how much personal timidity and vulnerability this masquerade of a sick hermit betrays!

#[[Nietzsche Attacking Spinoza]]

Although, we should note that in [[Beyond Good and Evil - What is Noble|What is Noble]] Nietzsche actually says that [[Beyond Good and Evil - What is Noble#^e2d4d7|solitude is a virtue]]. 

"Every great philosophy so far" is a "personal confession of its author" [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §6]. It is not a a "drive to knowledge" but some other drive that "employed understanding (and misunderstanding) as a mere instrument."

He does concede that there are some people who really have a drive for knowledge, without "essential participation from the other drives of the scholar." But this is a scientific kind of worldview for a specific thing. They could study biology, or they could study physics, or whatever. 

The philosopher can't separate themselves from their philosophy, though. Especially with morality. Especially with metaphysics.

![[Nietzsche criticizes Stoicism]]

People in Europe are still pondering the question of the thing-in-itself [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §10]. There are some people who seem to really want to have a "will to truth"—they won't believe in anything unless it's certain, even though they have a bunch of beautiful options that aren't certain. Nietzsche thinks this is nihilism. 

Even worse, when they speak against appearance, when they deny obvious things like the existence of their own bodies—they could just be using this as an excuse to get *more reactionary* [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §10]. 

> A little *more* strength, flight, courage, and artistic power, and they would want to *rise*—not return! 
> 
> [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §10]

Nietzsche says [[kant-immanuel|Kant]] just goes "oh lol how do we have synthetic a priori judgements? uh.... the synthetic a priori faculty!" [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §10]. He says the real question is not "how are synthetic judgements *a priori* possible," but "why is belief in such judgments necessary?" As in, why the fuck do we care?

There is no *atomic* soul, but just because it isn't *atomic* doesn't mean that there is no soul at all [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §12]. 

> Physiologists should think before putting down the instinct of self-preservation as the cardinal instinct of an organic being. A living thing seeks above all to *discharge* its strength—life itself is *[[Will to Power Mentions by Nietzsche|will to power]];* self-preservation is only one of the indirect and most frequent *results.*
> 
> In short, here as everywhere else, let us beware of *superfluous* teleological principles—one of which is the instinct of self-preservation (we owe it to Spinoza’s inconsistency).[18](file:///Applications/Koodo%20Reader.app/Contents/Resources/app.asar/build/index.html#c01_ft18) Thus method, which must be essentially economy of principles, demands it.
> 
> [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §13]

#[[Nietzsche Attacking Spinoza]]

Holy shit. He says it: "physics, too, is only an interpretation and exegesis of the world (to suit us, if I may say so!) and *not* a world-explanation" [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §14].

Nietzsche criticizes the view that our reality is both constructed by our organs and that our knowledge of reality is indirect [-@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §15]. He says it is absurd to say that we know both at the same time—since reality is the result of our perception, and we perceive the organs, so the perception of our organs are constructing themselves...

He thinks it's stupid to say "oh, *I thought this!*" as if the *I* were just able to determine itself nakedly [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §16]. When we say "I think," we have no idea:
- if it is *I* who think,
- that there must be something that thinks,
- that thinking is a thing that something causes,
- that there is an ego,
- or WHAT thinking is!

He says that implicit in the process is someone just asserting all of those things.

Thoughts come when "they" wish—not when "we" wish them [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §17]. Even then, it's not an "it" or a "they"—that's just an interpretation of the process.

Nietzsche *really* shows how he's broken from [[schopenhauer-arthur|Schopenhauer]] here [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §19].

> Philosophers are accustomed to speak of the will as if it were the best-known thing in the world; indeed, Schopenhauer has given us to understand that the will alone is really known to us, absolutely and completely known, without subtraction or addition. But again and again it seems to me that in this case, too, Schopenhauer only did what philosophers are in the habit of doing—he adopted a *popular prejudice* and exaggerated it. Willing seems to me to be above all something *complicated*, something that is a unit only as a word—and it is precisely in this one word that the popular prejudice lurks, which has defeated the always inadequate caution of philosophers.
> 
> [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §19]

Nietzsche gets really careful here and asserts that what "willing" really is [-@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §19]:
- the sensation of moving away from and to something, a muscular feeling, that begins as we "decide" to do it
- the thought of "willing"
- an *affect* of the command; the idea that it "is someone's"

[[Philosophy]] moves like en ecosystem; it's not that ideas build on each other per say; the ideas "always resolve once more in the same orbit" [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §20].

*Fascinating* point he raises, somewhat linguistically deterministic: the similarity of philosophy between Indian, Greek, and German comes from "unconscious domination and guidance by similar grammatical functions" [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §20].

Check out [[vico-giambattista|Giambattista Vico]] and [[von-humbolt-wilhelm]] on these points of [[linguistic determinism]].

Nietzsche thinks that people should be careful when thinking about "free will" and reduction mere "determinism" [-@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §21]. "Cause and effect" are "conventional fictions." People are too keen to either go "uh, no, I'm totally free! I can't handle not being free!" or "uh, I don't need to have any accountability, uh, everything's just determined."

He criticizes anarchists who say "neither God nor Master" and justify themselves upon an egalitarian image of nature [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §22]. You can tell this isn't really true because someone else could just look at the same phenomena of nature and conclude that it is incredibly *un*just and hierarchical!

> If, however, a person should regard even the affects of hatred, envy, covetousness, and the lust to rule as conditions of life, as factors which, fundamentally and essentially, must be present in the general economy of life (and must, therefore, be further enhanced if life is to be further enhanced)—he will suffer from such a view of things as from seasickness. And yet even this hypothesis is far from being the strangest and most painful in this immense and almost new domain of dangerous insights; and there are in fact a hundred good reasons why everyone should keep away from it who—can.
> 
> [@nietzsche_2010_good-evil-prelude, §23]
